LedgerLens is a working capital decision support application built for the DDL Labs assignment. It connects accounts receivable, accounts payable, collection activity and payment controls in a dashboard with a conversational Assistant. The reporting snapshot is 31 December 2025, and all monetary amounts are in Indian rupees. Fourteen CSV files contain 2,452 records. Importing them into an isolated PostgreSQL database produced no rejected records and two retained chronology warnings.

The application uses Next.js, React and TypeScript with PostgreSQL financial services. Source tables preserve original identifiers, dates, decimal amounts and CSV record locations. Migration checksums, transaction boundaries and validation make setup repeatable and failed snapshot replacement recoverable. A shared calculation determines outstanding amounts from original invoices, eligible allocated cash and signed adjustments. The same settlement policy reconstructs historical closes, so later receipts cannot silently rewrite earlier balances. Missing or inconsistent inputs remain unavailable instead of becoming zero.

At the cutoff, net receivables are ₹3,94,93,294.91 and net payables are ₹1,65,42,512.87. The resulting net trade balance is ₹2,29,50,782.04. Three customer receipts contain ₹85,000 in unallocated cash. Receivables overdue total ₹3,25,71,982.61. Capped disputed portions account for ₹92,635.23, leaving ₹3,24,79,347.38 for collection review. These are supported review amounts, not promises of recovery. Exception observations can overlap and must not be added as unique exposure.

December receivables reconcile from an opening ₹3,52,14,000.31, plus new postings ₹63,69,153.74, less applied receipts ₹20,89,859.14, with zero net adjustments, to the closing balance. Customer analysis distinguishes billing, received cash, invoice allocations, monthly due cohorts, cumulative historical shortfalls and current overdue. A separately labelled illustration continues three complete months of net movement; it does not claim to predict collections. Supplier review separates held or disputed obligations and treats recorded pending approval as a control exception.

The Assistant implements six scenario paths through validated backend financial tools. It resolves customer names against master records, retains customer, issue and period context, and retrieves live evidence for follow-up questions. Provider failures trigger bounded fallback rather than unverified financial text. Replies use deterministic service wording, with source links for inspection. Shared PostgreSQL reservations limit request rates and concurrency. The Assistant cannot initiate payments, change credit limits, write off debt or edit financial ledgers.

Verification includes thirty-eight isolated integration checks and twenty-seven CSV arithmetic assertions. Independent cent-based calculations, every daily and monthly bridge, missing values, large decimals, allocation conflicts, chronology, reference grounding, provider failures and cancellation are covered. Five Gemini tool calls passed; broader provider behavior and quotas remain unverified. Browser, mobile, keyboard, microphone and public deployment checks remain pending.

The dataset lacks inventory, COGS, separately identified net credit sales, bank statements and general ledger controls. Formal DSO, DPO, full cash conversion cycle and bank reconciliation are therefore unavailable. Supported billing-based days proxies disclose their denominator. Dispute status and MSME flags cannot establish missing historical resolution or legal acceptance dates. The recommended next step is an approved live walkthrough and source review before operational adoption.

Reviewers can trace document balances to invoices, cash allocations, adjustments and CSV records. Browser scenario evidence and presentation inspection require further work.
